# Knowledge quality principles

## Authority and provenance

Evaluate authority for each claim within its domain, version, and context. Cite the governing text for normative requirements and the relevant primary material for observations. Record the publisher or project, document or artifact, revision or edition, and a locator precise enough to verify the claim. Where a source cannot be pinned, record the access date and that limitation.

## Semantic consistency and reconciliation

Define terms, units, and version boundaries consistently within each index. Reconcile equivalent descriptions only when their meanings and applicability align. Preserve differences in scope, status, conditions, and behavior where they affect interpretation. Explain material mappings and document conflicts that evidence cannot resolve.

## Identities and history

Assign stable record identifiers with documented meaning. Preserve traceability when an identity is revised, merged, split, or retired, and reserve retired identifiers from reassignment. Version published records through identifiable releases. Preserve historical releases, the source revisions they used, and a record of material changes.

## Evidence and verification

Match the evidence to the claim. Identify the source that establishes a normative requirement, the reasoning behind an interpretation, and the environment and method behind an empirical observation. Consensus, prevalence, and observed behavior can provide evidence about practice. Normative authority must come from the governing source within its stated scope.

Keep claims independently checkable through citations, documented methods, and validation appropriate to the domain. For experiments, record relevant versions, inputs, conditions, and results. Where verification depends on restricted sources or unavailable environments, record the constraint and the evidence accessible to reviewers.

## Uncertainty and negative knowledge

Distinguish missing evidence, unresolved conflict, documented absence, and behavior that was tested and did not occur. Scope negative findings to what the source or method supports, including applicable versions and conditions. Distinguish an untested case from a tested result. Preserve conflicting accounts and state what additional evidence would resolve them where known.

## Transparent revisions

Record why a material claim or representation changed, what evidence informed the revision, and the versions affected. Preserve enough information to reconstruct the evidence and reasoning behind published conclusions.
