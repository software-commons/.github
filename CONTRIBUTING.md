# Contributing to Software Commons

Software Commons welcomes source-backed corrections, source submissions, and proposals for independently coherent knowledge domains. Review the [knowledge principles][principles] and [index policy][index-policy] before proposing a substantive change.

Follow the [Software Commons Evidence Standard][evidence-standard] for material factual claims. Provide exact source and claim records, actual research and independent verification artifacts, and qualification results when applicable. The [Codex Research Protocol][codex-protocol] governs agent research and review.

## Choose the issue location

- **Knowledge correction.** Use the issue tracker for the affected index. Include the record identifier or path, affected release, proposed correction, and evidence. For shared policy, identify the relevant section in the [organization repository][org-repository].
- **Source submission.** Use the relevant index's issue tracker. Describe the claims the source can support, its publisher and revision, its relationship to existing sources, and applicable access or reuse limits.
- **Index proposal.** Use the [organization issue tracker][org-issues]. Define the domain, exclusions, likely primary sources, and why the domain warrants independent publication. Describe overlap and how the proposal would respect existing record ownership.

Check for existing discussion before starting a new issue. Discuss changes to scope, identity, publication contracts, or shared policy before preparing a large pull request.

## Prepare a change

1. Keep the change focused and link any relevant issue or decision.
2. Cite sources precisely. Identify editions or revisions and the sections, records, or artifacts that support the claim.
3. Explain material interpretation or reconciliation choices. Record conflicting evidence, limits, and unresolved questions.
4. Run and report the repository's applicable validation. For empirical evidence, supply reproducible steps and record the environment, versions, inputs, and results.
5. Identify affected releases, record identities, and cross-index references. Describe any breaking change and available migration path.
6. Submit material you have permission to contribute. Describe relevant source terms and cite or link to material where redistribution is restricted.

Follow the [code of conduct][conduct] and any repository-specific contribution guidance. Repositories may adapt the defaults to their domain and methods.
